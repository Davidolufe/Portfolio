Management findings and recommendations

Across 720 cases, confirmed first-contact resolution was 49.7%, repeat case rate was 26.8%, and complaint rate was 20.7%. Average initial resolution time was 22.52 hours.

  1. Prioritise billing and technical support. Together these categories account for 419 cases (58.2% of demand). Billing has a 38.1% repeat rate; technical faults have a 36.8% repeat rate. Review examples before deciding why customers repeat contact.
  2. Technical faults take longest to resolve. Their average initial resolution time is 32.48 hours, compared with 29.77 hours for billing. A diagnostic checklist and named ownership are proposed for a pilot.
  3. Investigate live queue coverage. Average phone/chat waits rise to 10.93 minutes in August and 10.71 minutes in September. There are 120 cases in every month, so these data do not establish that higher case volume caused the delay.
  4. Check October's resolution delays. Initial resolution averages 30.11 hours in October, the highest monthly value. Inspect issue mix and unresolved handoffs; neither is established as the cause.
  5. Keep survey coverage visible. Average CSAT is 3.91/5 from 556 responses (77.2% coverage). Non-response could affect interpretation.

Process improvement proposal

The AS-IS flow is an assumed baseline for this case study, rather than an observed operational process. The TO-BE flow is a proposed design.

AS-IS · assumed baseline

Customer raises an issue → case is logged
↓
Agent investigates using available information
↓
Resolved? Close case. Unresolved? Transfer to another queue.
↓
If the issue recurs, the customer contacts the company again

TO-BE · proposed process

Log issue and check for related repeat contact
↓
Use a billing guide or technical diagnostic checklist
↓
Resolved? Confirm understanding. Unresolved? Assign owner and update date.
↓
Give a clear summary; monitor seven-day repeat contact and outcomes

Pilot acceptance criteria

  • Every unresolved pilot case has a named owner and promised update date.
  • Dashboard totals and rates reconcile to the case dataset.
  • Email wait and missing satisfaction responses remain blank.
  • Compare four pilot weeks with four baseline weeks, using the same case mix and definitions.
  • Assess repeat rate, FCR and complaints together; do not claim success from handling-time reductions alone.

Owner and promised-update fields would need to be added to the operational system; they are not present in the supplied historical dataset.

Definitions and limitations

  • Grain: one row per case. Follow-up contacts are a count attached to the case; this is not an interaction-level log.
  • Confirmed FCR: initial resolution marked Yes and zero repeat contacts within seven days, divided by all cases.
  • Repeat case rate: cases with one or more repeats divided by all cases. This differs from total repeat contacts per case.
  • Resolution hours: elapsed hours to the initial resolution; repeat activity may occur afterwards.
  • Complaint rate: cases marked Complaint = Yes divided by all cases.
  • CSAT: average score from 1–5 among survey responses. Response rate is responses divided by cases; non-response may bias the average.
  • Live wait: average initial queue wait across phone and web chat cases. Email is excluded because email response time was not supplied.
  • Observation: assume records were extracted on 7 November 2026 or later, allowing seven-day follow-up for all October cases.
  • Limitations: all data and patterns are synthetic; no customer ID, revenue, arrival timestamps, agent roster or verified root-cause fields. Six months of equal case counts cannot establish real demand growth or annual seasonality.
